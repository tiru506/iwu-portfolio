Understanding the infrastructure behind generative AI

How Large Language Models Are Trained

From collecting data to deploying a model, training a large language model takes more than writing code. It needs high-quality data, powerful computers, electricity, skilled people, time, and careful testing.

01 · Introduction

What does it take to train an LLM?

A large language model (LLM) learns statistical patterns from large collections of information so it can generate useful responses. Training is repeated, technically demanding, and resource intensive.

📚

Data

Text, code, images, and other data are collected, filtered, organized, and prepared.

🖥️

Compute

Groups of specialized processors perform enormous numbers of mathematical operations.

⚡

Energy and time

Servers use electricity and cooling. Large training runs can take days or weeks of coordinated work.

Important: Exact training costs for many leading commercial models are not public. This graphic separates published figures from information that developers have not disclosed.
02 · Training process

From raw information to a deployed model

Training is a sequence of stages. Each stage needs data, people, software, and computing resources.

Collect data

Gather suitable text, code, images, or other information from permitted public, licensed, and curated sources.

Clean and prepare

Remove duplicates and low-quality material, apply filters, check quality, and convert information into tokens.

Pre-train the model

Train the neural network on large datasets. A common objective is predicting the next token in a sequence.

Fine-tune and align

Use instruction examples and human or AI feedback to improve helpfulness and instruction following.

Evaluate and improve

Test accuracy, reasoning, safety, bias, and reliability. Address problems and repeat selected steps.

Deploy and monitor

Serve the model to users, monitor performance and safety, maintain infrastructure, and release updates.

Training vs. use: Pre-training and fine-tuning create or adapt a model. Deployment is where the model answers requests. Serving millions of requests also consumes compute and energy.
03 · Resources and costs

What resources are needed?

The investment includes more than electricity. Hardware, data rights, engineering, experiments, networking, storage, and cooling all contribute.

🗂️

Data

Collection, licensing, filtering, annotation, storage, and quality review.

🧠

Compute

GPUs or other accelerators, servers, fast networking, and cloud or data-center capacity.

⚡

Energy

Electricity for processors, memory, networking, cooling, and supporting systems.

⏱️

Time and people

Training runs, experiments, engineering, research, safety testing, and maintenance.

How the costs add up

Budgets can include accelerator rental or depreciation, data acquisition, staff, storage, networking, cooling, electricity, failed runs, and evaluation. Public reports rarely provide every cost category.

Simple energy calculation

Energy depends on average power draw and operating time.

Energy (kWh) = Average power (kW) × Time (hours)
Electricity cost = Energy (kWh) × Price per kWh

05 · Key takeaways

What should we remember?

1. Data quality matters

Large datasets need filtering, documentation, and responsible handling. More data does not automatically mean better data.

2. Compute is a major investment

Large runs require many accelerators working together, supported by fast networks, storage, and reliable infrastructure.

3. Energy has an environmental impact

Electricity demand and emissions depend on hardware efficiency, data-center design, cooling, and energy sources.

4. Training is one part of the lifecycle

Fine-tuning, evaluation, deployment, and repeated user requests all require resources.

My main takeaway: Training generative AI is a large engineering effort, not just a software task. Performance depends on data, computing resources, and careful testing. Costs also include energy, time, people, and the infrastructure needed after training.
